Li and Yuan conjectured that a ternary cyclic code of prime length avoids full-weight codewords exactly when 0 is outside its spectral support and at most half of the nonzero cyclotomic cosets are inside it [[1]](#ref-1).
The code below meets both conditions at length 109 and contains a word whose 109 entries are all nonzero. Exactly nine cyclic codes at this length contain no full-weight word.
A team of AI agents in my research lab found the word in a 0.9-second search, the lab's fixed checker (one the agents cannot change) verified it, and the run billed USD 0.38.
You can check the word yourself: a button under [Why it is right](#why-it-is-right) runs the check in your browser, and the same check is a 35-line Python file.
The search method was not theirs, and the one error that reached the checker was an array an agent copied by hand.

## The conjecture

Fix a prime $n > 3$ with $f = \operatorname{ord}_n(3)$ odd.
The cyclotomic coset of $s$ is $C_s = \{s, 3s, 9s, \dots\} \bmod n$; the $r = (n-1)/f$ nonzero cosets each have $f$ elements.
With $\zeta$ a primitive $n$-th root of unity in $\mathbb{F}_{3^f}$, a word $c \in \mathbb{F}_3^n$ has spectrum $\widehat{c}_j = \sum_i c_i \zeta^{ij}$, and $\mathcal{C}_S$ is the cyclic code of words whose spectrum vanishes outside $S$, a union of $t$ cosets.
$\mathcal{N}(n,3)$ is the set of these codes with no full-weight codeword, no word with every $c_i \neq 0$.

Conjecture 4.2 of [[1]](#ref-1) says $\mathcal{C}_S \in \mathcal{N}(n,3)$ if and only if $0 \notin S$ and $t \le r/2$, and it implies their Conjecture 4.1, $|\mathcal{N}(n,3)| = \sum_{j \le r/2} \binom{r}{j}$.
The paper proves the case $r = 2$ and checks $n = 13$ by enumerating every cyclic code; it was posted on 2 July 2026 and has one version.

Since $f$ is odd, negation sends each coset to a different one, its mirror.
The paper's Theorem 4.2 rules out full-weight words when $S \cap (-S) = \emptyset$: such a word has $(c, c) = n \neq 0$, while that spectrum forces $(c, c) = 0$.
One mirror pair escapes the argument and still has $t \le r/2$ when $r = 4$.
After $n = 13$, the smallest prime with $f$ odd and $r \ge 4$ is $n = 109$: $\operatorname{ord}_{109}(3) = 27$, and the cosets are $C_1, C_2, C_4, C_8$ with $-C_1 = C_4$ and $-C_2 = C_8$.

## Why it is right

You do not need $\mathbb{F}_{3^{27}}$ to check it.
Since $\widehat{c}_j = c(\zeta^j)$ for $c(x) = \sum_i c_i x^i$, the spectrum vanishes exactly where $\zeta^j$ is a root of $g(x) = \gcd(c(x), x^{109} - 1)$, a polynomial over $\mathbb{F}_3$:

1. $\deg g = 55$, and since $3 \nmid 109$ the roots of $x^{109} - 1$ are distinct, so the spectrum has exactly 55 zeros.
2. Roots over $\mathbb{F}_3$ are closed under cubing, so the zeros form a union of cosets, and $55 = 1 + 27 + 27$ forces $\{0\}$ and two nonzero cosets.
3. $g$ equals its own reciprocal up to a scalar, so its roots are closed under $j \mapsto -j$, and the two cosets are a mirror pair.
4. The spectrum is nonzero on exactly the other mirror pair: $0 \notin S$, $t = 2 = r/2$, and every entry of $c$ is 1 or 2.

<details class="ui-disclosure">
<summary>Why the paper's own theorems do not rule it out</summary>

Theorem 4.3 of [[1]](#ref-1) could have forbidden this code, and does not reach it.
It needs every nonempty union of cosets $T \subseteq S$ to satisfy (1) $T \cap (-T) = \emptyset$ or (2) some $\ell \neq 0$ with exactly one representation $\ell = a + b$, $a, b \in T$.
For $T = S$ both fail: $S = -S$, and every nonzero $\ell$ has 13 or 14 representations.
</details>

## Why the exact count is nine

Yangcheng Li independently checked the witness and pointed out how it gives the exact count in Conjecture 4.1 at length 109. If the support $S$ contains frequency zero, its code contains the all-one word. If $S$ contains either whole mirror pair, its code contains the corresponding full-weight word constructed above. Enlarging the support preserves that word.

This leaves supports that omit zero and choose at most one coset from each mirror pair. Their codes are self-orthogonal because $S$ and $-S$ are disjoint. A ternary word of weight 109 has self-inner product $109 \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$, since each nonzero entry squares to one. Such a word cannot belong to a self-orthogonal code.

For each of the two mirror pairs we may choose either coset or neither. These choices give exactly $3^2=9$ codes, including the zero code. This count attains the lower bound in Theorem 4.2. Conjecture 4.1 predicts $1+4+6=11$ and fails at this length.

## How the agents found it

A lead and a writer ran GLM-5.3 in opencode, an open-source coding agent, each in its own sandbox.
Their output is a claim: a short write-up with the codeword attached, which the fixed checker decides in three stages: format, mathematics, and novelty against dated sources.
The operator is the Claude Code session that runs the lab for me; it watches runs from outside and can message a team.

The method was the operator's.
A 2-hour pilot the day before ran annealed walks toward fewer zero entries and stalled at 7 zeros, which says nothing: a random-like $[109, 54]$ code has about $9 \times 10^{14}$ words with 7 zeros and about $3.7 \times 10^{6}$ with none.
The operator wrote that count into the run's brief with the method: a full-weight word is nonzero on every information set, so walking all $2^{53}$ sign patterns on 54 information coordinates passes every one, and the first should come after about $(3/2)^{55} \approx 5 \times 10^{9}$ steps.
Before the run, the brief recorded 0.35 as the chance of a verified refutation within 4 hours.

All times are UTC on 17 November 2026; the run started at 19:44.
The lead [chose an exhaustive Gray-code walk over more annealing](/research/li-yuan-full-weight?pursuit=claim-cyclic-full-weight-20261117b&event=60df273d9cf1:30574#agent-record), built the code "exactly as the checker constructs it (same irreducible modulus, same ζ)", and [validated the search at $n = 13$](/research/li-yuan-full-weight?pursuit=claim-cyclic-full-weight-20261117b&event=60df273d9cf1:51760#agent-record), where the paper's enumeration gives the answer.
At 20:03:59 the C search on 8 cores [printed](/research/li-yuan-full-weight?pursuit=claim-cyclic-full-weight-20261117b&event=60df273d9cf1:51978#agent-record) `FOUND thread 5 outer_step 9058516 lane 21 full=1 sum=0`, after about $1.8 \times 10^{9}$ patterns and 0.9 seconds, earlier than expected about one time in three.
Within 25 seconds the lead had recomputed the word's spectrum and a copy of the checker in its sandbox had accepted it.

Then a typo cost thirty minutes.
The first submission to the checker, rc1, failed at 20:16 with `artifact.codeword must be 109 entries in {0, 1, 2}`.
The lead's local copy passed, so it [concluded](/research/li-yuan-full-weight?pursuit=claim-cyclic-full-weight-20261117b&event=60df273d9cf1:66525#agent-record) that "the corruption happens in the Lab's unwrap/collector", re-typed the word as a compressed string, mistyped that too, and told the writer the pipeline had mangled the array.
At 20:28 the operator sent one fact checked outside the run: the array in the write-up had 110 entries, and that, not the lab, failed rc1.
The lead [agreed](/research/li-yuan-full-weight?pursuit=claim-cyclic-full-weight-20261117b&event=60df273d9cf1:70298#agent-record), found the line now held 112 entries, watched a code-generated paste come out at 110, and [stopped](/research/li-yuan-full-weight?pursuit=claim-cyclic-full-weight-20261117b&event=60df273d9cf1:73972#agent-record): "hand-copying long arrays is unworkable."
A second message fixed the citations (the brief's source said conj3.2; the PDF prints Conjecture 4.2) and asked for the Theorem 4.3 check, which the lead [did](/research/li-yuan-full-weight?pursuit=claim-cyclic-full-weight-20261117b&event=60df273d9cf1:90776#agent-record) by 20:40.
The second submission, rc2, [passed all three stages](/research/li-yuan-full-weight?pursuit=claim-cyclic-full-weight-20261117b&event=60df273d9cf1:94258#agent-record) at 20:46; the run settled at 20:52, 68 minutes after it started.

Five agents took part: the lead, three spawns of the writer (the first never started; the other two lacked tools their setup asked for) and a helper the last writer spawned to read the write-up back.

## What it says about research agents

A person with the method note and a 200-line script could have done this in an afternoon.
The hard step was the count that said where the words are and why annealing could not find them, and it came from outside the team.
The agents did the tedious part well: field arithmetic matched to the checker, validation at the one length where the answer is known, and no claim until the checker agreed.
They failed in a specific way: a low-cost model copied 109 numbers by hand, got 110, and blamed the infrastructure with confidence.
The fixed checker refused the wrong word, and the operator, reading from outside the run, found the cause and sent it in one message.

So if you build a research-agent team, spend on the checker and the method before the model, and give the agents one hard rule: every number a claim carries is produced by code, never typed.

## Status

I sent the witness to the authors on 17 November 2026 and received their response the following day. At this length, each of the nine codes without a full-weight word satisfies both spectral conditions in Conjecture 4.2.
