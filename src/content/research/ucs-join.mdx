Pálvölgyi and Tardos found a concept class whose smallest unlabeled compression scheme is larger than its VC-dimension [[1]](#ref-1).
They conjectured that joining copies of that class makes the gap grow linearly.
A 386-entry table shows that two copies already close it.

## Conjecture

A concept class $F$ is a set of functions from a domain $B$ to $\{0,1\}$.
An unlabeled compression scheme of size $k$ (Definition 5 of [[1]](#ref-1)) is a map $\beta$ from subsets $Y\subseteq B$ with $|Y|\leq k$ to arbitrary functions $B\to\{0,1\}$, such that for every $f\in F$ and every sample $S\subseteq B$ some $Y\subseteq S$ has $\beta(Y)$ equal to $f$ on $S$.
The sample is compressed to the unlabeled set $Y$, and $\beta$ alone recovers every label in $S$.
$\mathrm{UCS}(F)$ is the smallest such $k$, and $\mathrm{UCS}(F)\geq\mathrm{VC}(F)$.

The class $C_5$ consists of the functions on $\mathbb{Z}_5$ that take the values $1,0,0,1$ on some four consecutive vertices: the ten rotations of $11100$ and $10100$.
It has $\mathrm{VC}(C_5)=2$ and $\mathrm{UCS}(C_5)=3$.
The join $F*G$ is the class of pairs $(f,g)$ on the disjoint union of the two domains; VC-dimension adds under joins, and $\mathrm{UCS}(F*G)\leq\mathrm{UCS}(F)+\mathrm{UCS}(G)$.
Pálvölgyi and Tardos proved $\mathrm{UCS}(C_5*C_5)\leq5$ and stated

> **Conjecture 10.** $\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{UCS}(C_5^{*n})/n$ exists and is strictly larger than 2.

They also asked whether $\mathrm{UCS}(F*G)\geq\mathrm{UCS}(F)+\mathrm{UCS}(G)-1$ always holds.

## Counterexample

The public repository [[3]](#ref-3) gives $\beta$ on all 386 subsets of the ten points of $C_5*C_5$ with at most four elements.
Each of the 30,976 pairs $(S,f|_S)$ with $f\in C_5*C_5$ has a covering $Y\subseteq S$ with $|Y|\leq4$.
Here $S$ ranges over all $2^{10}$ subsets of the domain, of every size up to 10; the count is $176^2$ because the samples of a join are pairs of samples of the two copies, and $C_5$ has 176 distinct pairs $(S,f|_S)$ over the 32 subsets of $\mathbb{Z}_5$.
Definition 5 asks nothing more: $\alpha(g)$ may be any covering $Y$, and $\beta$ need not be injective or return a function of the class.
So $\mathrm{UCS}(C_5*C_5)\leq4$, and the bound $\mathrm{UCS}\geq\mathrm{VC}=4$ makes it equal.
Since $4<3+3-1$, the answer to the question above is no.

For the limit, both bounds are short.
A shattered set of size $d$ has $2^d$ traces, and two traces cannot share an address, so a scheme needs $k\geq d$; hence $\mathrm{UCS}(C_5^{*n})\geq2n$.
Decoding the halves of a join separately gives subadditivity, so $C_5^{*2m}=(C_5*C_5)^{*m}$ has a scheme of size $4m$, and one more copy of $C_5$ adds 3:

$$
2n\;\leq\;\mathrm{UCS}(C_5^{*n})\;\leq\;2n+(n\bmod 2).
$$

The ratio tends to 2, so Conjecture 10 is false.

The table has no description shorter than itself.
The run that rechecked it also showed why: a size-4 scheme cannot decode one copy from that copy's points alone, so every optimal scheme must use which points of the other copy were kept.

## Scope

The result refutes Conjecture 10 as stated and removes joins of $C_5$ as a route to an unbounded gap.
It does not decide whether $\mathrm{UCS}-\mathrm{VC}$ is bounded, the central question of [[1]](#ref-1), and it does not bear on the sample compression conjecture [[2]](#ref-2).
Whether $\mathrm{UCS}(C_5^{*3})$ is 6 or 7 is open.
A search on October 7, 2026 of the citing works indexed by Semantic Scholar and OpenAlex, with full texts read where available, and of the web found no earlier statement of this result; two paywalled citing papers were not read, and MathSciNet and zbMATH were not searched.
Every check here was written by Claude models.
No outside expert has reviewed the result, and no peer review is asserted.
