The cube map on $\mathbb{F}_{32}$, composed with one linear bijection, is an APN permutation whose reduced polynomial has no critical point.
That refutes Conjecture 2 of Bartoli and Stănică [[1]](#ref-1), and with it the normalized form of a lifting conjecture of Rønjom and Sandrib [[2]](#ref-2).
The counterexample checks by hand.

## Conjecture

Let $q=2^m$.
A function $F:\mathbb{F}_q\to\mathbb{F}_q$ is APN (almost perfect nonlinear) when, for every $a\neq0$ and every $b$, the equation $F(x+a)+F(x)=b$ has at most two solutions.
Every such function is induced by exactly one polynomial of degree below $q$, its reduced representative $f$.
A critical point is an element $a\in\mathbb{F}_q$ with $f'(a)=0$, where $f'$ is the formal derivative.

Conjecture 2 of [[1]](#ref-1), the Reduced Critical-Point Conjecture, states that for every $q=2^m$ the reduced representative of every APN permutation of $\mathbb{F}_q$ has a critical point in $\mathbb{F}_q$.
The paper's Theorem 2.2 shows that this is equivalent to its Conjecture 1: no coefficientwise lift of that reduced representative permutes the Galois ring $\mathrm{GR}(2^k,m)$ for any $k>1$.
Conjecture 1 is the normalized form of Conjecture 2 in [[2]](#ref-2).
The paper proves the statement for monomials and cubic polynomials and leaves other families open.

## Counterexample

Over $\mathbb{F}_{32}$, let

$$
L(x)=x+x^{8}+x^{16},\qquad F(x)=L(x)^{3}.
$$

$L$ is $\mathbb{F}_2$-linear, and its associated polynomial $1+t^3+t^4$ is coprime to $t^5+1=(t+1)(t^4+t^3+t^2+t+1)$, so $L$ is a bijection.
The Gold map $x^3$ is an APN permutation of $\mathbb{F}_{32}$ because $\gcd(3,31)=1$.
Composing it with a linear bijection on the input keeps both properties.

On $\mathbb{F}_{32}$ we have $x^{32}=x$, so $L(x)^2=x^2+x^{16}+x$ and

$$
\begin{aligned}
F(x)&=(x+x^{2}+x^{16})(x+x^{8}+x^{16})\\
&=x^{24}+x^{18}+x^{10}+x^{9}+x^{3}+x^{2}+x.
\end{aligned}
$$

The two $x^{17}$ terms cancel and $x^{32}$ reduces to $x$.
No modulus enters the calculation, so this is the reduced representative $f$ under every choice of irreducible quintic.
In characteristic two only the odd exponents survive differentiation:

$$
f'(x)=x^{8}+x^{2}+1=(x^{4}+x+1)^{2}.
$$

The polynomial $x^4+x+1$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{F}_2$, so its roots lie in $\mathbb{F}_{16}\setminus\mathbb{F}_2$.
Since $\mathbb{F}_{16}\cap\mathbb{F}_{32}=\mathbb{F}_2$, $f'$ has no root in $\mathbb{F}_{32}$, and the APN permutation $F$ has no critical point.
By the paper's Galois-ring permutation criterion (its Theorem 2.1), every coefficientwise lift of $f$ then permutes $\mathrm{GR}(2^k,5)$ for every $k>1$, which refutes Conjecture 1 directly.

## Why relabelling breaks it

Section 7 of [[1]](#ref-1) treats power maps: for $f=ax^e$ with $e$ odd, $f'(0)=0$.
Relabelling the output by an $\mathbb{F}_2$-linear map $B$ keeps that critical point, because no exponent of $B(x^e)$ reduces to $1$ unless $x^e$ is itself linear.
A field-affine map $x\mapsto ax+b$ on either side keeps a critical point or moves it.
A linear relabelling of the input mixes the Frobenius powers $x^{2^i}$ before the power map acts, and the critical point can disappear.
Having a critical point is a property of one representative, not of its equivalence class.

The audit measured how often this happens.
Random two-sided $\mathbb{F}_2$-affine relabellings of APN power maps had no critical point 370,430 times in 1,000,000 at $m=5$, 817,835 times in 2,200,000 at $m=7$, and 242,811 times in 650,000 at $m=9$.
That is about 37%, near the $1/e$ rate the audit also found for random permutations.
Output-side and field-affine relabellings never did, in 3,850,000 samples each.

At $m=3$ the conjecture holds: all 10,752 APN permutations among the 40,320 permutations of $\mathbb{F}_8$ have a critical point.
$\mathbb{F}_4$ and $\mathbb{F}_{16}$ have no APN permutations, so $m=5$ is the smallest field with a counterexample, setting aside $\mathbb{F}_2$, where every map is affine.

## Scope

Conjecture 2 of arXiv:2608.30808v1 is false as stated, already at $m=5$, and so are its Conjecture 1 and the normalized Rønjom–Sandrib conjecture.
The witnesses are relabelled textbook power maps, so none is a new APN function, and the failure of critical points to survive relabelling is not claimed as new.
Corrected forms of the conjecture were not studied.
A search on September 27, 2026 of arXiv, IACR ePrint, Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex and the web found no earlier counterexample; recent, unindexed or private work cannot be excluded.
No peer review is asserted.
