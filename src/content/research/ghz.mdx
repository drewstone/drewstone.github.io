A mixture of a four-qubit GHZ state and two basis states violates the BCWW candidate inequality [[1]](#ref-1) by $0.113302\ldots$ bits.
The relevant marginal entropies are classical, so this construction does not establish a quantum advantage.

## State and marginal entropies

Define

$$
|\mathrm{GHZ}_4\rangle=\frac{|0000\rangle+|1111\rangle}{\sqrt2},
\qquad
\rho=\frac34|\mathrm{GHZ}_4\rangle\!\langle\mathrm{GHZ}_4|
+\frac18|1010\rangle\!\langle1010|
+\frac18|1001\rangle\!\langle1001|.
$$

The [BCWW functional](/research/bcww/#inequality) uses only proper subsystems.
Tracing out a nonempty complement removes the GHZ off-diagonal term.
Every required entropy therefore equals the Shannon entropy of the corresponding marginal of

$$
P(0000)=P(1111)=\frac38,\qquad
P(1010)=P(1001)=\frac18.
$$

With logarithms in base two, substitution gives

$$
F=\frac{25}{4}-\frac98\log_2 3-\frac{15}{8}\log_2 5
 =0.113302008774\ldots>0.
$$

The exact sign certificate is

$$
2^{50}=1125899906842624>600677490234375=3^9 5^{15}.
$$

The retained standard-library verifier reconstructs each marginal and checks the closed form [[2]](#ref-2).
Its October 2 replay succeeded.

## Attribution and retained attempts

The provenance note credits the fifth arm of an operator verification workflow dated August 10, 2026.
That workflow's five Claude Code sessions survive [[3]](#ref-3), and the literature-audit session is the arm that found this state.
Its responses report the model `claude-fable-5`.
Asked to find any published refutation, confirmation, or discussion of the inequality, it found none.
It then varied the weights of the Ingleton-violating mixture of Linden, Matúš, Ruskai, and Winter [[4]](#ref-4), whose weights $(\tfrac12,\tfrac14,\tfrac14)$ satisfy the bound.
At 04:17 UTC on August 11 it evaluated $p=3/4$, $q=r=1/8$ at $+0.113302$ bits; the largest value it found in that family was $+0.114511$ bits near $p\approx0.773$.
Its report that the state is new reflects that search, not a proof of first discovery.
The other four sessions checked the five-atom witness, the transcription, and the scope of the inequality.
The operator session that launched the workflow is not published.

The August 30 repair pursuit [[5]](#ref-5) later revisited the witness.
Its director's OpenCode session survives as the execution bridge's event stream: assistant text and tool inputs, without prompts, tool results, or reasoning.
Its inputs named the witness only by the value $+0.113302$ bits, so it reconstructed the state by scanning GHZ-plus-basis mixtures, and its knowledge-base call wrote the finding shown in that record.
Both children ran in remote sandboxes whose sessions were not retained; they ended down after rate-limit and incomplete-stream failures.
The director's stream ends after thirteen consecutive provider usage-limit errors.
Neither record certifies complete capture.
This is a second witness to the [same BCWW refutation](/research/bcww/).
