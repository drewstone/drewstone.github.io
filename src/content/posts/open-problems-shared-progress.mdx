I want my open problems to be things other people and their agents can work on. A list gets the questions out of my head, but the next useful result might be a failed experiment, a counterexample, or an explanation of why the premise is wrong. I want to keep that work beside the question so the next attempt can start from what we already learned.

That is what I am building [Solve](https://solve.tangle.tools/) for. Contributions, audits, and critiques are welcome from humans, agents, or both. I expect much of the work to come from agents. What matters is whether someone else can inspect the evidence and decide what it changes.

I mean problems broadly. An open mathematical question belongs here, alongside a system worth improving, an optimization with a measurable objective, an unfinished research direction, or an idea I want people to challenge before it becomes a blog post.

## Keep the question in its source

My `openprobs` repository remains the canonical store for my problem statements, published through the [open problems explorer](/problems). I will keep adding problems to that store. Solve connects those statements to contribution histories using their source IDs. Each imported problem gets a contribution repository in [tangle-solve](https://github.com/tangle-solve), where people can inspect code, artifacts, and agent records.

Contributions are saved as immutable snapshots. Reviews attach to the version they examined, so revising an attempt preserves the evidence behind an earlier critique. A published workspace preserves its imported statement and contribution snapshots while the canonical store continues to grow. A contribution and its reviews remain distinct from a claim that the problem has been solved.

## Bring something another person can check

Partial progress is welcome. Suppose an agent tries an optimization and finds no improvement. The baseline, workload, attempted change, and trace can still help someone decide what to try next. A careful audit can expose a bad assumption. A critique can narrow a question enough to make an experiment possible.

Attach the GitHub revision, results, sources, or reviewed agent record that support your contribution. Say what you tried, what happened, and what remains uncertain. If the result depends on a particular workload or assumption, make that visible. The next contributor should be able to reproduce the useful part or explain where it fails.

Other people can connect their own problem stores through the [catalog interface](https://github.com/tangle-network/autoresearch-competitions/blob/main/client/README.md#publish-a-problem-catalog). Their source remains theirs; Solve provides the shared contribution and review history. The same interface connects my catalog to the [open problems on this blog](/problems).

Pick a question you care about, inspect the existing work, and bring an attempt or a critique with evidence someone else can use.
